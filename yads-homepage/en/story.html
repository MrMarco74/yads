Building with AI: A New Approach

After years of postponing this project, the emergence of advanced AI coding assistants like Google Gemini and Claude presented an opportunity I couldn't ignore. I thought: "Let's see how far one can get with well-designed prompts."

The approach was unconventional. First, I mapped out the architecture, defined core functionality, and created detailed technical requirements. Then, instead of writing code myself, I handed these specifications to the AI assistants for implementation.

The entire codebase was created without me writing a single line manually. My role was purely strategic: designing prompts, conducting user acceptance tests, performing functionality checks, and refining implementation details.

Were there challenges? Absolutely. I even got temporarily blocked by Google for 48 hours due to excessive API usage. But the results speak for themselves: when you understand how these AI tools work and invest time in crafting precise prompts, the possibilities are remarkable.

Can AI Replace Developers?

No, but I see a fundamental shift coming for many in this field. The future belongs to those who master prompt design, quality assurance, and customer communication. The role is evolving from "writing code" to "directing code creation."

"As someone living with Parkinson's disease, this approach was a game-changer. Using brief sentences or voice chats with AI assistants allowed me to be productive even on difficult days. It transformed how I work and what I thought was possible."

The Original Vision

The idea started simple: create a tool where you input a list of domains, and it automatically discovers all related assets belonging to that organization.

The Evolution of Features

From that initial concept, the scope grew organically:

Domain Discovery - Finding all domains and subdomains connected to a company
TLD Analysis - Checking if all top-level domain variants (.com, .net, .de, etc.) are owned by the company or still available
Typosquatting Detection - Identifying domains that could be used for phishing attacks
Web Server Probing - Checking if servers respond to HTTP and whether they properly redirect to HTTPS
SSL/TLS Analysis - Verifying certificates and checking encryption configurations
Brand Monitoring - Finding domains using company logos that might be unauthorized or malicious
Web Scraping & Secret Detection - Identifying forms, APIs, and exposed sensitive data
Security Configuration Checks - Testing for OWASP vulnerabilities

Throughout this development, one principle remained constant: stay under the radar. All scanning is designed to be passive, rate-limited, and non-aggressive - avoiding detection by web application firewalls and anti-DDoS systems. In Germany, we must always be mindful of Section 202c StGB (the "Hacker Paragraph").

Seeing the Big Picture

A key insight came when asking: "What does an organization's entire DNS universe actually look like when visualized?"

The answer was fascinating. Organizations typically have a main "domain galaxy" with interconnected assets, but also many isolated domains floating freely - often forgotten, abandoned, or created without IT oversight. This visualization became a powerful tool for understanding attack surface and identifying housekeeping opportunities.

Later additions like Nuclei vulnerability scanning and Nmap port scanning (using slow, stealthy approaches for the top 1000 ports) further expanded the platform's capability to paint a complete picture of an organization's external attack surface.

What's Next?

Some features are already implemented, others are on the roadmap:

  • Multi-tenancy & Licensing - Already available for enterprise deployments
  • SIEM Integration - Splunk connectivity is built in
  • Privileged Access Management - CyberArk integration for DSM/PSM is planned
  • Ticketing Integration - Connect to Jira or ServiceNow, with automatic ticket creation and rescan triggers when issues are resolved
  • Distributed Scanning - Multiple worker nodes scanning from different IPs, enabling threshold discovery scans that slowly increase request rates until detection, then back off and retry from fresh IPs
  • Reputation Checks - Integration with services like VirusTotal

The Road Ahead

Freemium? Open Source? Acquisition by a larger company?

Honestly, I don't know yet. At its core, this is a passion project - though I've deliberately built in the foundations to take it further if the opportunity arises.

Who among us doesn't dream of having an idea good enough to provide more freedom in how we spend our time? At the same time, I genuinely enjoy working on this - learning new technologies, exploring new tools, and seeing the platform evolve.

Since I use YADS myself and have already discovered real issues with my own domains, I'm committed to continuing its development for the foreseeable future. The journey continues.
